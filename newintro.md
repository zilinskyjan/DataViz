LLMs can be excellent teachers

	- They "know" a lot (loosely speaking)
	- They are patient ("let's go back", "explain it differently...")
	- The level of an explanation can be dialed up/down

---

AI tools can be excellent teachers

	- They are multimodal
	- You can speak and explain what exactly you know and where you get lost

---

Open question: Can they spark curiosity?

Or will they encourage users to delegate too much?

(probably depends on the context, the user's personality, etc.)

---

Risks

	- A perfectly frictionless intellectual environment could stunt your growth
	- It could systematically shield you from genuinely uncomfortable ideas
	- (Might prevent serendipitous intellectual discovery if the memory feature does a lot of work; but this seems less of a risk when a conversation is started from scratch and you use API or create a new account.) 

---

Brief reflections on (AI-driven) echo chambers

- The status quo is already flawed: People actively curate their own reality every day.
- Seeking comfort and avoiding cognitive dissonance is not a moral failing; it's a fundamental aspect of human psychology.

*Given this reality, why should we see the AI version of this problem as uniquely dangerous?*

:::small
I believe the difference lies not in the impulse (which is timelessly human), but in the efficiency, perfection, and nature of the tool we are building to satisfy that impulse.
:::small
